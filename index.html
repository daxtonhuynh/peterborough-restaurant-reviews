BEYOND THE STARS.

WHAT DO 7,073 REVIEWS REVEAL ABOUT PETERBOROUGH'S RESTAURANTS?

Peterborough diners are overwhelmingly happy: 89% of reviews are 4–5 stars. So this project asks what goes wrong in the rest. An NLP pipeline reads 7,073 Google reviews of 25 restaurants sentence by sentence, measures sentiment on 15 aspects of a visit, links complaints to 1–2 star ratings, benchmarks every restaurant against peers that operate like it, and turns the gaps into action plans.

01 / The question

Diners are happy. Ratings are decided by the failures.

Aspect-based sentiment · Rating drivers · Peer benchmarking · Action plans

7,073Google reviews analysed
89.2%of reviews rated 4 or 5 stars
18.6%of text reviews contain at least one complaint

25 Peterborough restaurants, Jan 2024 – Jun 2026; about 41% of the reviews listed on Google.

A star average tells an owner how visits went, not why.

The restaurants in the sample average 4.57 stars. A number that high and that stable is almost useless as a management signal: an owner at 4.5 cannot tell from it whether the problem is the kitchen, the floor or the wait.

The reasons are in the text. 5,165 reviews carry written comments, split here into 17,191 sentence units. The pipeline reads every unit, tags what it is about, from food and staff to order accuracy and complaint handling, and whether it is praise or a complaint.

That turns free text into three things an owner can use: what customers talk about, which complaints go with 1–2 star ratings, and where a restaurant stands against peers that run the same kind of service.

What made it hard

No labelled data

There was no ground truth for restaurant aspects. The categories had to be discovered first (BERTopic found 268 topics), fixed in a codebook with rules for the hard cases, and only then measured.

A strongly positive sample

With 89% of reviews at 4–5 stars, the useful signal sits in a thin tail of complaints. Rare aspects such as order accuracy or complaint handling appear in under 3% of text reviews.

Two judgements in one sentence

"Great food but slow service" praises one thing and criticises another. Sentiment had to be scored per aspect, on the clause that holds it, and 578 run-on sentences joined by ".." had to be split first.

Fair comparison

A fast-casual counter and a premium dining room fail in different ways. Each restaurant is compared only with its own operational peer group, with itself left out of the peer rate.

What the analysis found
12.3percentage points higher chance of a 1–2 star rating when a review complains about cooking execution

Execution sinks ratings

Freshness, temperature and doneness complaints carry the strongest link to a low rating, ahead of staff (+9.9 pp), cleanliness (+8.0) and waiting (+6.5). Food is mentioned in 86% of text reviews; how it leaves the kitchen is what costs stars.

74%of reviews with three or more complaints are 1–2 stars

Problems compound

With no complaint, only 1.7% of reviews are 1–2 stars. One complaint lifts that to 27%, two to 57%. A good recovery after the first problem keeps it the only problem.

35%of evaluative mentions of waiting are complaints

Waiting is the weakest big aspect

Speed & waiting has the lowest net sentiment of the frequently mentioned aspects (+0.30), and its complaint rate rose from 4.4% to 5.6% of text reviews in the last 12 months.

50%of 1–2 star reviews get an owner reply

Half of unhappy reviewers hear nothing back

Complaint handling is rarely mentioned but almost always negative when it is, and it edged up in the last 12 months (0.7% → 1.2% of text reviews).

02 / Findings

What the reviews say

+Food and people are the strengths. Food is praised in 75.6% of text reviews (net +0.81) and staff in 51.6% (net +0.87); named servers are a recurring reason for 5-star reviews. Ambience, menu variety and dietary options are rarely criticised.
−Waiting, execution and getting the order right are the weak spots. Speed & waiting draws complaints in 5.0% of text reviews, the highest rate after food; dry, cold or soggy food follows at 3.9%. Order accuracy and complaint handling are almost never praised.

What is changingLast 12 months vs everything before

  • Waiting complaints rose from 4.4% to 5.6% of text reviews (z = 1.94).
  • Complaint handling rose from 0.7% to 1.2% (z = 1.86).
  • All negative judgements rose by 1.2 pp, the one change that clears the 95% threshold on its own (z = 2.07).
  • Price, ambience and cleanliness complaints did not rise.
  • The slide is concentrated in fast casual: three of the four biggest rating declines are high-volume counter-service restaurants, while most full-service restaurants held steady.

How each layer works

The technical choices behind the findings

Data

One clean table, nothing deleted

Dates and IDs standardised, HTML and URLs stripped, emoji and punctuation kept, raw and cleaned text side by side. 1,905 star-only reviews stay in the rating analysis; 38 non-English reviews use Google's translation; 84 suspicious duplicates are kept out of labelling. Each restaurant is assigned by rule to a peer group from its category, price range and service style.

Segmentation

Sentence units, not whole reviews

spaCy splits sentences, then clauses around contrast words (but, however, although). 578 sentences that were really several joined by ".." or "…" are split only where a capital letter follows, so lowercase continuations stay whole. Every unit keeps its review ID and character offsets.

Topic discovery

BERTopic finds the taxonomy; it does not label

all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings with UMAP and HDBSCAN: 120 topics on all sentences, 48 on 1–3 star reviews and 100 on 4–5 star reviews. Used as a classifier, topics alone reach only 0.383 micro F1, which is why they feed the codebook rather than the final labels.

Codebook

18 categories, with rules for the hard cases

Each category has a definition, inclusion cues, exclusion rules, real examples and a KPI link, plus 24 annotation rules for multi-aspect sentences, sarcasm and comparisons. An external review of v1.0 led to a re-mapping that changed 34 primary aspects; v1.2 settles boundaries such as "an accepted long wait" (Speed, neutral) and "feeling rushed" (Staff).

ABSA model

Transparent by design

An aspect is present if its cue lexicon fires or a TF-IDF (1–2-gram) one-vs-rest logistic regression gives p ≥ 0.5. Sentiment is VADER with domain terms, scored only on the clause that holds the aspect, then adjusted by aspect rules. Star ratings are never an input, so the rating models are not circular.

Rating drivers

Three models, one answer

An ordered logit on 1–5 stars (pseudo R² 0.266); a logit for 1–2 stars with restaurant fixed effects and cluster-robust errors, reported as average marginal effects (pseudo R² 0.57); and gradient boosting as a non-linear check (test AUC 0.948). All three control for review length and year.

From benchmark to priority

How a restaurant's gaps become a ranked action plan

BenchmarkPer restaurant × aspect

Negative and positive rates as a share of the restaurant's text reviews, each with a Wilson 95% interval, compared with its peer group (itself excluded) by a two-proportion z test.

Pairs with at least 4 negative reviews go on to scoring

Rescaled to a 0–100 index; 100 = the highest pair in the city

Action planUp to five items

79 items across 25 restaurants (73 Priority, 6 Watch), graded 13 High, 42 Medium and 24 Low confidence. Finding, evidence and quotes come from the data; the action and KPI come from a 14-aspect playbook and are a starting point for the owner.

The rulesStrength · weakness · confidence

  • Strength: at least 8 positive reviews, a positive rate at least 2 pp above peers and net sentiment of at least +0.6.
  • Weakness: at least 4 negative reviews, a negative rate at least 1 pp above peers and z ≥ 1.28.
  • Priority item: one of the top-scoring aspects with a negative rate at or above the peer rate; otherwise one Watch item.
  • Confidence: High when n ≥ 15 and z ≥ 1.64, Medium when n ≥ 8, otherwise Low.

04 / What owners can do

Remove the failures, not polish the average

Because most reviews are already 4–5 stars, the cheapest rating gains come from removing what pulls a visit down to 1–2 stars. Each point is a hypothesis for an owner to test against a KPI, not a measured effect.

1Control execution at the pass. Freshness, temperature and doneness complaints have the strongest link to low ratings and are rising. Hold-time limits, pass checks and better takeout packaging are low-cost fixes.
2Protect the service moment at peak. Waiting is the most negative big aspect and is rising; breakfast and family diners see it most (8.5% of text reviews). Honest wait quotes and peak staffing matter more than speed on quiet days.
3Stop small problems stacking up. Ratings fall sharply once a second issue appears. A good recovery after the first problem keeps it the only one.
4Lean on people as the differentiator. Named staff are the most common reason for praise after food; recognising and keeping them is a competitive asset.
5Answer complaints. Complaint handling is rare but almost always negative and rising, and only half of 1–2 star reviews get an owner reply.

05 / Limits

How far to trust the numbers, and how to make them stronger.

The pipeline is built so that better labels flow straight through: verify the pilot, measure agreement, re-run steps 07 to 09. Until then, read the findings as consistent signals from what reviewers wrote.
